v5.0.0 is live

v5.0.0 of File-Organizer-MCP is officially live on npm. But the real reason this release exists is basically an accident of timing.

A few days ago, Z.ai quietly dropped their GLM-5.3-Flash model under stealth as Ox-Alpha. Along with it came a massive flood of free tokens. At the time, I was locked in on building Blueline in Rust, but when you are sitting on a mountain of free inference, it starts burning a hole in your pocket. I wanted an excuse to see what the model could actually do on messy, real-world code.

Around the same time, the whole timeline was losing its mind over Bun using Claude to rewrite 500,000 lines of Zig into Rust for $165,000. I looked at that and thought, why shouldn't I do a rewrite of my own? Except I did not need a $165,000 bill or a Rust ego trip nor did i have that. What I actually needed was to open up my first real open-source repo and aggressively gut the over-engineered TypeScript Frankenstein I built when I was first learning.

Joining the cool kids club

There is this running joke in tech that senior engineers measure their value by how much code they delete. You hear people talk about PRs with negative ten thousand lines like it is the official gateway into the cool kids club.

Except in my case, there was no 20-year staff engineer carefully pruning a monorepo. It was just a degenerate vibe-coder larping as a dev, feeding messy prompts into Ox-Alpha at 2 AM and watching thousands of lines of boilerplate get deleted in real time.

And honestly, it felt incredible. The original repo had ballooned into nearly 35,000 lines spread across 27 separate services. When you are learning Node and TypeScript on the fly, your immediate instinct is to create a brand new service class for every single shower thought. You wrap basic filesystem calls in three layers of architecture because you think that is what serious software looks like. Deleting whole directories of that fluff and watching the codebase shrink down to raw, focused tools was pure relief.

Fuck around and find out, carefully

I didn't have a bunch of users ready with machetes on my neck. It was just a fat-to-fit transformation for the codebase.

At the same time, this isn't a frontend web app where a bug just means a button renders funny. An MCP server has permission to move real files on your actual hard drive. It was mainly the fear of me being responsible for someone running a rm -rf /* on their machine, basically. If a filesystem tool glitches, it eats your documents or overwrites a project you spent three months building.

I was so paranoid about security and regressions that I had automated path traversal fuzzing and race-condition gates running on every change. I am one of the 7% gigachads who use Linux (arch btw) so people on Mac and Windows might not get the same native love, but that's what GitHub issues are for :/

The anticlimactic npm saga

Right before shipping, I thought I had hit a genuine wall. During my migration from Windows to Linux, I managed to lose access to my original npm account. I had already accepted my fate. I assumed the account was gone forever, that I would have to fork my own package, publish v5 under some garbage alternate name, and strand existing users on 3.4.2.

I had mentally prepared for a whole administrative headache. Then I hit "forgot password", checked my email, and got the account back in thirty seconds lol. No drama, no fork, same package name.

Your code isn't as important as you think

If there is one piece of advice I took away from this whole rewrite, it is that your code isn't as important as you think.

When you start out, you fall in love with your own complexity. You get emotionally attached to your 27 services, your clever helper classes, and your shiny abstractions. You convince yourself that writing more lines of code means you are doing real engineering. But nobody is framing your TypeScript files on their wall.

Your agents don't think that your 15 abstractions for a simple read_file() are cool, they'll shame you if they had access to social media. The question you should ask isn't "Can I afford to make my code simpler", the question is "Can you afford not to?"

v5.0.0 is live on npm. 22 tools, zero session baggage, and a codebase lean enough that I can actually read it without getting a headache.

npm install -g file-organizer-mcp

Point your client at it and go clean something up.
